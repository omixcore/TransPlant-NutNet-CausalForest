# Cover Letter — Global Change Biology

Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript "**Downslope transplantation increases plant community turnover while mean richness remains uncertain**" for consideration as an Original Research article in *Global Change Biology*.

Mountain plant communities are widely reported to be reorganising under climate change, yet the evidence is dominated by resurvey and observational studies in which climate tracking cannot be separated from land-use change, nitrogen deposition and recording artefacts. Transplant experiments were designed to break exactly this confounding — treatment is assigned by the experimenter, and paired in-situ controls share site, substrate, history and observer — but their results are typically reported as a single average richness response, which conflates transient transplant shock with persistent warming effects and conceals the compositional processes beneath a near-zero net number.

Using the TransPlant network (20 mountain-grassland regions, nine countries, 41 destination sites, 225 transplanted turfs, 1,171 turf-year observations, 2007–2022) and generalised random forests, we convert this randomised but heterogeneous experiment into three quantities that species richness alone cannot deliver:

1. **A demographic mechanism.** Warming accelerates colonisation (+1.18 species per turf-year) and extinction (+0.92) with an unresolved difference between component effects; total compositional turnover rises by +2.08 species per turf-year (p < 0.001) while the net richness estimate remains uncertain (separately fitted forest models; component additivity is imperfect). "No net change" does not mean "no effect": composition is put on a fast conveyor belt while its species count stands still.

2. **A quantified climatic lag.** The community temperature index tracked only **2.9% (approximate 95% CI 0.004–5.9%, conditional on fixed mean dose)** of the imposed +2.4 °C warming. The directional outcome did not survive correction across six outcomes, and the phase contrast has not been directly tested.

3. **An honest heterogeneity verdict.** Regional point estimates differ in sign (−3.98 to +3.38), without a direct between-region test, but no covariate moderator survives multiple-testing correction; we report this split verdict explicitly rather than over-claiming predictable winners and losers.

We believe this work fits GCB's scope in three ways. It addresses a **global-change driver** (experimentally imposed warming) with a **mechanistic, process-level link** to community response (the colonisation–extinction accounting, with a transplant comparison, a within-site placebo diagnostic, a separate Nutrient Network fertilisation positive control, and an anti-circularity design for the thermal-preference estimates). Its conclusions are **not site-specific**: the network spans nine countries across Asia, Europe and North America, and the finding that *averages mislead* — different regional point estimates, decoupling of flux from net change — is itself the generalisable message for the large body of distributed warming experiments whose data already exist in public archives. Finally, the framework is immediately reusable: all data are public (OSF, EDI), the analysis scripts and recorded outputs are available on GitHub and Gitee; source data must be obtained from the cited archives.
