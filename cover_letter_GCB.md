# Cover Letter

Dear Editors,

Please consider “Downslope transplantation increases plant community turnover while mean richness remains uncertain” as a Research Article in Global Change Biology.

## 1. Scientific question

Does transplantation of intact mountain grassland communities to warmer sites change species richness, colonisation, extinction, and thermal composition, and do effects vary among communities and years?

## 2. Key findings

Across 1,171 turf-years in 20 regions, transplantation increased colonisation by 1.18 and extinction by 0.92 species per turf-year, producing a 2.08-species increase in turnover. The mean richness effect was +0.65 species with a 95% interval from −0.39 to 1.69.

## 3. Importance and timing

Species counts can miss rapid replacement. A geographically distributed transplant experiment now permits direct estimates of the processes underlying richness change. The results foreground species turnover as a measurable response to changing conditions at warmer sites.

## 4. Fit with Global Change Biology

The paper tests a global change intervention across mountain grasslands in nine countries. It combines experimentally assigned transplantation, species-level community data, and causal effect estimates to quantify how community composition changes under warmer destination conditions.

## 5. Biological and global change aspects

The biological outcomes are colonisation, extinction, turnover, richness, and community temperature index. The global change exposure is movement of intact grassland turfs downslope to warmer sites, compared with paired in-situ controls. The estimated turnover effect is the central result; thermal directionality remains unresolved after multiple-outcome correction.

## 6. Three recent relevant publications

Korell et al. (2026), Ecology, doi:10.1002/ecy.70486; Feeley et al. (2026), Global Change Biology, doi:10.1111/gcb.71063; Wootton et al. (2026), Oikos, doi:10.1002/oik.11978. These address warming, turnover, thermophilization, and elevational context.
